# Molecules

What is not a linear polymer of letters: a ligand, an ion, a drug, a glycan, and the modified residues inside a polymer.
Seq\* holds **pointers and labels, never chemistry**: a component code, a SMILES or a GlyTouCan id is a string it
matches and shows; parsing and depicting chemistry is a host's. Built in the fourth slice (language 0.5.0).

## Kinds

- A **molecule** sits beside a sequence: its positions are its components (one for ATP, several for a glycan), it
  carries identifiers as open words (`ccd:ATP`, `smiles:…`, `glytoucan:…`), and optionally its heavy atoms as a second
  form. Its residues and atoms are joined by a many-to-one map applied only at a path's ends.
- A **modified residue** keeps its parent's letter (MSE draws as M, PSU as U) and carries its component, marked on the
  letter and named on hover; several components at one position are all kept. A modification known by name only (a
  UniProt PTM) is a `modified` range, found by the same query: `(filter :test (= (prop modified) true))`.
- A **complex** has **units** (chains and molecules in a structure, each with its author numbering); copies are derived.
  Its **links** (a drug on a lysine, a glycan on an asparagine, a disulfide) are a pairs node.

## Queries

A structure's parts are selected with one record, as MolViewSpec and MolQL write it:
`(elements :component HEM :atom FE)` gives 4HHB's four irons, `(elements :chain A :beg 10 :end 20 :atom CA)` eleven of
CDK2's Cα atoms. The finest field says how deep it goes: chains, residues or atoms. `(groups …)` gives one key per
residue (or atom, unit, molecule), and a filter tests any property. Chains and molecules as nodes stay `(chain …)` and
`(molecule …)`.

## On an axis

A modified residue is drawn as its parent's letter with a mark: a dot at the cell's corner by default (a tick above the
strip when zoomed out), or its code in place of the letter (as Mol\*'s sequence panel), or an outlined cell, or nothing;
the track's choice. None of these widens a cell: the default is compact. A row of markers by kind, as UniProt's feature
viewers draw, is a ranges track over the sites.

A molecule is a chip in every layout by default (its label, a fixed slot); its residues or atoms by patch, which changes
that one block while the rest of the section still follows the complex. A section of a complex lays out polymers first,
then molecules; water is left out unless asked. Where a molecule binds, and whether hovering it lights its linked sites,
is the application's to turn on.

## Reading

From the `seq` format, and from mmCIF through an opt-in runtime on `@molstar/io`'s reader. Sharing the parsed
representation with a host such as Mol\*, rather than parsing again, is a design of its own: the seq data ports.
