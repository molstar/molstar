# Pairs, grids and rows

Four things a user reads off a family or a molecule: **pairs** (a tRNA's base pairs, contacts across chains), **grids**
(a value per pair of positions: a base-pair matrix, a PAE), an alignment's **rows**, and its **logo**. What is drawn
depends on the pixels on screen, not the amount of data.

## Pairs

- A pair is two ranges, on one system or two, with a kind. Drawn as arcs over a track, chords through a ring's hole, or
  dots on a grid, by the same layer.
- Nested arcs nest; crossing pairs (pseudoknots) are drawn crossing, or the crossing set on the other side
  (`crossing: split`). An end past the window is reached a quarter window past the edge, so arcs move smoothly.
- Hover or select a pair and both ends light, here and on linked sections. Past a few thousand arcs in view they are
  binned by the screen, and the view says so.

## Grids and dot plots

- A grid is a section of its own with two axes, `x` and `y`, each able to follow another section: a PAE between two
  chains has its x follow one chain's section and its y the other's.
- Hover is a **crosshair**: both positions light on the grid and on every linked section. A drag selects a
  **rectangle**: two ranges at once; Cmd/Ctrl+drag adds one, Alt+drag removes one.
- Upper and lower triangles may hold different data (`triangle`); `mirror` draws a pair both ways.
- Zoomed out, a grid draws from a reduction to the pixel, never empty while a tile is built.

## Rows

- An alignment's rows over its columns, ordered by data, by tree or by a field of the rows' table; filtered, hidden (a
  "3 hidden" chip shows them again) and pinned to a band on top.
- Each row's first and last residue numbers, beside it, are those of the residues in view: they follow a pan or zoom.
- The rows scroll inside their band while the ruler and logo above stay put; `]` and `[` zoom the rows, `0` fits rows
  and columns.
- 10,000 rows are held and 10 million drawn lazily at the same frame time; a row not yet loaded shows as pending.
- Clicking a row's header selects it, Shift and Cmd/Ctrl extend and toggle; a drag selects a block of rows × columns; a
  click or drag on the ruler's upper part selects columns (its lower part is the ruler rail and pans).
- The keyboard cursor steps cells and rows, a page at a time, over rows never loaded.
- **Row columns**: a row's annotations (species, description, length, identity to the reference, a field joined from a
  file) as columns on the left of the alignment or on its right, as text, numbers, bars or chips; their titles above the
  rows order the rows by them, and they give way to a narrow view before the labels do.

## Clades

- A click on a tree node folds or unfolds its clade into a **summary row**: "first +n" beside a wedge, its cells the
  clade's per-column consensus. Hovering a node or a summary row lights the clade: its branches and its rows' band.
- A fold is **session state**, like the scroll: no undo step. `clades.keep` writes the folds shown into the spec as its
  opening state, one edit. Collapse all, expand all, collapse to a depth or a distance cut are commands; a drag of the
  cut line across the tree is too.
- Folding never changes the selection: a selection holding part of a clade shows as a partial mark on its summary row.
- Focusing a row inside a folded clade, following a link to it or landing on it in a search opens its clades; back folds
  them again.
- In a text figure every row shows on every line, unfolded.

## The logo

Each column's letters stacked by information content (bits) or probability, from the alignment's profile. A click
selects the column. Its letters keep their scheme's hue, darkened or lightened to read on the page in either theme: the
scheme's pale cell colors are for cells, not glyphs.
