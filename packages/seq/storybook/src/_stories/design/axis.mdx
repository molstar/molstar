# The axis

A section lays out one coordinate system across the page: its axis. The axis has a direction, may carry another system's
letters through a map, and may hide stretches such as introns. Hit-testing, selection, follow, focus, the view's history
and the export all read positions through it, so what is drawn, what is hit and what is exported agree.

## Direction

A section's `direction` is the writer's choice, never read off its layers:

| Value       | Reads                                                                                           |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `genomic`   | positions count up left to right (the default for a section that follows nothing)               |
| `reversed`  | positions count down                                                                            |
| `follow`    | along its leader: a minus-strand gene following its protein reads N to C (a follower's default) |
| `{ along }` | along a named system: a gene section reading along its protein                                  |

- A reversed axis says so: the ruler counts down and the section's label carries a direction mark, in the export too
  (`axis.directionMark`; a host whose own chrome says it sets `none`).
- Letters read against their strand on screen show as their complement by default (`complement: auto`); `always` and
  `never` are the writer's. A protein is never complemented, and the stored letters are what search and status read.
- Navigation is in display order, whatever the direction: a drag, the wheel and the arrows move what is on screen the
  way the hand goes.
- A direction that cannot be resolved is reported and the section reads `genomic`; a path with runs both ways reads in
  the direction of most of its length.

## Pieces: collapsed introns

- `pieces` says what part of the system a section shows: `whole` (the default, for every section), `coding` (the coding
  runs of a codon map), a ranges node, or a list of them (two isoforms' exons). `pad` keeps a few positions each side of
  every piece (splice sites).
- Where pieces leave positions hidden, a **break** is drawn: a hairline across the section and a notch on the axis edge,
  zero width on every layout, rings included. Its hover names the hidden length and how many items it hides.
- What a break hides is not lost: items wholly inside it draw one tick on the break, and a selection or hover there
  lights the break.
- `section.collapse` (Shift+C) toggles a section between `whole` and its `collapsed` form; `section.reverse` (Shift+R)
  flips its direction. Each is one edit of the spec, one undo step. Expanding a single intron is not offered: the export
  would then differ from the screen.

## Letters through a path

A section can carry another system's letters when the path pairs positions one to one (an alignment row, an offset) or a
residue to a codon. So a gene section can show its protein's letters, each residue three nucleotides wide, a codon split
by an intron drawn in each part. Three-letter codes show where the narrowest part has room (`code: auto`), and a residue
its codon does not translate to is drawn in the conflict color. Other paths (a nucleotide on a protein's axis) are
refused with the reason.

## What reads the axis

- **Selection**: a click on a residue selects the residue, projected onto its codon. A drag across a break selects only
  the positions shown (`selection.acrossBreaks: shown`; `span` adds the hidden stretch).
- **The cursor** moves by a codon on a residue row, and announces a break it crosses.
- **Follow**, both ways: a gene following its protein on `coding` pieces moves exactly three units a residue, so it
  never crosses a hidden intron; moving the follower moves its leader.
- **Focus** on something wholly hidden moves to its break and says why.
- **History**: back and forward land on the same positions whether the axis changed or not: a step is restored by
  position, clamped to what is drawn, never dropped. Changing the direction or the pieces is an edit, so undo returns
  it.
- **Export**: the SVG is the canvas's picture, and an export's viewport may be given in positions.

## One axis for a protein and its gene

The figure "the protein and its gene under each other" is one gene section with the protein's letters and tracks through
the codon map, written `direction: { along: p53 }` and `pieces: coding`. The two-section view, the gene following the
protein, stays beside it.
