# Structure syncing

A sequence view beside a structure viewer: hover, selection and focus cross both ways, and a track's colors can be
painted onto the structure. Seq\* imports nothing of the structure viewer and the structure viewer nothing of Seq\*; the
application that composes both connects them through one port.

## Words that cross

Seq\* speaks its own locations: positions on the **units** of a complex (a chain instance, a ligand). The application's
adapter translates them into its viewer's words (MolViewSpec selectors, Mol\*'s loci) and back, with the unit facts
Seq\* gives it: chain, operator, observed residues and author numbering. Unobserved residues are never sent, and a
selection too large to send residue by residue becomes whole units.

## The paths

- **Hover** lights the same residues in both views. Hovering one copy in 3D lights that chain and its reference
  sequence, never the sibling copies.
- **Selection** made in 3D replaces the sequence selection and pans to it, keeping the zoom.
- **Focus**: a double-click moves the camera, and back and forward bring it along, on the same clock as the sequence
  view's own motion.
- **Color by this track** is an action a host adds to a track's header: Seq\* returns the track's colors as runs on the
  units, and the host builds its viewer's state from them. It is a snapshot; clicking again restores the colors.

## Two maps of one chain

A reference sequence can reach a chain through SIFTS and through an alignment at once. Each map may carry a
**priority**: the higher-ranked one is taken everywhere. A section may name the map it goes **via**, so a figure can
compare both side by side. Equal priorities stay ambiguous and are reported, never chosen silently.

## In the Storybook

The structure viewer here is a small fake: chains as rows of residue cells, taking the same messages a real viewer
would, with an optional latency. A real 3D viewer will use the same port and the same tests.
