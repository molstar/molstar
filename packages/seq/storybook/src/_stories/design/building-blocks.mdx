# Building blocks

The nouns of the language. They are the first proposal: names may still change until the first examples run.

## Data

- **Spec**: the root. Data is defined once; views refer to it.
- **Coordinate system**: where positions live. A system has a length (or is open, for a streamed genome), may be cyclic,
  and has an alphabet when it carries letters. Positions are indices, 0-based; ranges are half-open. Labels (residue
  numbers, insertion codes, chromosome names) are converted at the edge.
- **Structure**: a chain instance of a structure is a system too. Its residues are keyed by the structure's numbering
  and flagged observed or not. A structure runtime (Mol\*) binds it to atoms.
- **Sequence**: a system with letters (a protein, a nucleic acid, a chromosome), shared by its **units** (chains,
  copies, haplotypes). Units are grouped by a **complex**. A location on one unit never reaches another unit through the
  sequence they share.
- **Map**: a directed relation between two systems. Three questions are answered separately.
  - **Kind**, what it means:
    - `identity`;
    - `offset`;
    - `numbering` (Kabat, IMGT, labels);
    - `segments`;
    - `pairwise` (a CIGAR);
    - `alignment` (a row onto columns);
    - `codon` (exons and a strand);
    - `structure` (a sequence onto a chain's residues, like SIFTS).
  - **Sourcing**, where it comes from:
    - inline, in the kind's compact form;
    - a source, read by region;
    - computed by a runtime the application provides. A codon map computed from a gene model is still a codon map.
  - **Encoding**, how it is held, the cheapest that works:
    - implicit (arithmetic);
    - runs (a 400-residue protein on 5 exons is 5 runs);
    - expanded per position, only for the region asked.

  Direction (+1 or −1) is part of every run, so an inversion, a reverse-strand gene and a protein on its codons are one
  mechanism.

- **Path and projection**: maps compose into paths, and a location is carried along the shortest unambiguous path.
- **Alignment**: rows, each with its map onto a columns system.
- **Annotation**: data on a system, in one of six shapes:
  - `ranges`: intervals with fields;
  - `values`: a number per position or bin;
  - `pairs`: two locations, possibly on two systems;
  - `matrix`: a value per row and position;
  - `grid`: a value per pair of positions;
  - `rows`: keyed records.

  It reads from a **source**.

- **Location**: a set of ranges on one system. It is what a query returns, what hover and selection hold, and what focus
  names.
- **Query**: a small composable language whose result is a location. It has generators, modifiers (by label, expand,
  onto another system), filters and combinators.

## Views

- **View**: sections laid out as tracks or as text, opening on a **focus**.
- **Section**: one axis (a system, or a piece of one: a range, reversed, with gaps collapsed) and its **tracks**.
- **Track**: a stack of **layers**.
- **Layer**: one way of drawing one annotation, or the letters: `letters`, `ranges`, `values`, `pairs`, `matrix`,
  `logo`, `ruler`, …. Each layer type declares its fallback, the simpler layer a reader without it draws instead.
- **Theme**: how a channel (color, size) is computed. It can be uniform, from a field's values (a palette, binned or
  continuous, over a domain that may be left open to the data in view), from threshold rules, or from a named scheme.
  Themes are data, never code.
- **Snapshot**: a named patch over the spec, such as a story's step or a bookmark.
- **Issue**: `{ code, severity, path, message, hint }`, with a stable code, from every pass. Issues are reported, not
  thrown.
