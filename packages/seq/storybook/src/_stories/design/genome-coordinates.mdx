# Genome coordinates

A genome is an **assembly**: its sequences, named, ordered, with their aliases. A section `of` an assembly shows its
chromosomes as **blocks** end to end, in a row or on a ring; a section `of` a list of assemblies shows each as a
**group**. A position is always a system's, whatever a block's direction or scale: nothing stored or exported is in
display order or in display units.

## A direction and a scale per block

- **`block.direction`**: `section` (the default) or `reversed`, against the section's direction, so `section.reverse`
  turns every block (the block order is kept). A reversed block's ruler counts down, its label carries the mark
  (`chr4 ←`), its letters are complemented under `auto`, and on a ring it runs counterclockwise within its arc.
- **`block.scale`**: its display units are its positions times its scale, so a region takes more of the row or the
  circle. Its label says so (`chr17 ×10`) and its ruler counts its own positions at its own density.
- **An inversion between genomes** is a block cut at its edges and reversed: B in A's order is explicit blocks (the
  builder's `colinearBlocks`), exported as drawn. A direction per piece inside one block stays open.

## Gaps at scale, and runs

- **One rule for rows and rings**: the gaps in view share at most a quarter of the length, each at most the layout's gap
  (12 px; 1° on a ring), so at 3,000 blocks a ring's gaps are 0.03° each and three quarters of the circle stays
  positions. A gap between two assemblies is wider (3 block gaps; 4° on a ring), taken first.
- **Small blocks run together**: consecutive blocks each narrower than 2 px are one band ("2,976 contigs"), no gaps
  inside. Inside a run a block is still read and drawn while it is a device pixel wide; under that its stretch shows the
  run's band alone. Runs are found again on a zoom, never on a pan.

## Places and units

Each section of blocks keeps an index of the blocks that show a system, so a position on a system has its **places**:
every block that shows it (overlapping blocks: each). A viewport, a history step, a saved view and an export's viewport
carry `{ system, position }` ends, never units. Two sections with equal block lists (a ring of the genome and its linear
detail) follow each other by identity; unequal lists do not follow.

## A hue per block

Every mark on a block has the built-in fields `block` (its label) and `system` (its sequence's name). A categorical
theme over them takes the section's blocks in order as its domain (over an assembly its names in its order, before any
`include`), so a sequence has one hue on every section of its assembly.

## Navigators and the viewer

- **A navigator over blocks**: with no `system` over a section of blocks, or `system` an assembly, it draws the genome's
  blocks; its window is the section's, carried by `{ system, position }` (one window over a gap). A click on a
  chromosome is a jump there, a double-click fits it (again: the whole); zoomed in, the zoom strip shows the chromosome
  holding the window. The readout and the slider name the block and the assembly
  (`chr17: 7,668,402–7,687,550 of GRCh38`).
- **`navigator.switch: include`**: a click on a chromosome shows it alone on the bound section (a ring of one chromosome
  is the genome's ring with `include` patched: one undo step); Home shows every one again.
- **The square**: a navigator on both axes over blocks × blocks, the chromosome pairs a lattice, the window one
  rectangle. What its cells hold (pairs by block, a contact map's tiles by block pair) is region reads'.
- **The viewer**: `navigate.acrossBlocks` (`cross`, or `stop`: one chromosome at a time); `block.next`, `block.previous`
  (Alt+PageDown, Alt+PageUp); `block.reverse` and `section.include` from a block's menu ("Reverse", "Show only", "Show
  all"); the keyboard cursor crosses into the next block (EC-154, amended); a drag across blocks selects each block's
  shown positions; the view's history keeps `{ system, position }`; a run's hover names its block ("one of 2,841"); the
  locus box falls back to a source's gene names.

Data over an assembly is read by sequence: a block reads its own sequence's rows (inline rows split by their `target`,
or by `a_name`/`b_name`, `x_name`/`y_name`, `name` for pairs, grids and matrices; a source by region under the names it
lists). Not yet built: the square's cells (region reads) and its grips; a navigator over a list of assemblies; the
whole-assembly level cut into block pairs.
