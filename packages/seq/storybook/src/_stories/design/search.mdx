# Search

A reader looks for a motif: a restriction site on a plasmid, a primer on a genome, a glycosylation motif in a protein,
a pattern across the rows of an alignment. Every hit is lit in the view, a count says where the reader is ("2 of 14"),
keys step through the hits, and the hits can be kept as a set to come back to.

## The library's part and the application's

Search is application-level work on a base built for it. The library runs the matchers and holds the result: the
query, the hits, the current hit and the status, with commands to run, step, go to a hit, clear and save. The search
box and a results list are the application's: these stories use a small box built only on the public surface, to show
that the surface is enough.

## Three matchers

- **IUPAC** (the default): a pattern letter stands for its set (`R` is A or G, `N` any base; on proteins `X` any
  residue, `B` D or N, `Z` E or Q, `J` I or L). A sequence's own ambiguity letter is a hit only where every base it may
  be is allowed: a site reported on a run of `N` is not a site.
- **Exact**: each letter itself.
- **Regular expression**: letters, `.`, classes, groups, alternation and repeats, never backreferences or lookaround.
  It runs in time linear in the letters whatever the pattern, so a pattern from a link can never hang the page.

All three ignore case (a genome's lowercase is soft-masking), treat U as T on nucleotides, skip an alignment's gaps,
search both strands of a nucleotide sequence (a site the same on both strands is one hit), and find a hit across a
circle's origin.

## What is searched, and what is shown

The whole of every sequence the view draws, not only what is in view, or what the search is limited to (an address, or
the selection). Hits are a highlight of their own (a tint) and the current hit an outline of the same color; they light
linked sections as any highlight does (a protein's hits on its gene). Stepping reveals a hit, panning only; the view's
Back returns. The selection stays the reader's.

## Bounded and said

Every bound is checked before the work it sizes: a pattern's length, a regular expression's size, the letters a run
reads, the work it takes and the hits it holds. Past the hits it holds the count goes on, so "182,331 hits" tells the
reader to narrow the pattern rather than showing a quiet 10,000.

## Keys

`Mod+G` and `F3` step to the next hit, with Shift to the previous one, while the view has the focus; `Mod+F` stays the
browser's or the host's own box.
